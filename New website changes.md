# About Page

## You love your dog. That doesn't mean living with them has to be exhausting.

A lot of the people I work with aren't dealing with a "bad dog."

They're dealing with a dog they love who pulls them around the neighborhood, gets overly worked up, reacts to other dogs, or brings a level of chaos into everyday life that they're tired of managing.

After a while, it wears on you.

You start planning walks around avoiding problems. You feel embarrassed when your dog acts out. You become tense because you're always wondering what's going to happen next.

My goal is to help change that.

Not by promising you a perfect dog, but by helping you build the communication and skills that make your dog easier to understand—and easier to trust.

### Clarity builds trust. Trust creates freedom.

That's the idea behind the way I train.

When expectations aren't clear, dogs are left guessing. Owners are left guessing too.

So we start by improving communication.

I use training, play, movement, leash work, and real-world situations to help owners become clearer with their dogs and help dogs understand what choices are available to them.

I don't want your dog simply learning what **not** to do.

I want them learning what they **can** do instead.

Over time, those clearer choices create something much more valuable than a rehearsed command:

**trust.**

Your dog begins trusting your guidance, and you begin trusting their decisions.

That's where greater freedom becomes possible.

### I didn't set out to become a dog trainer.

My interest in dog training started after I came home from the military and moved back in with my parents for about a year.

They had two dogs, and I liked taking them for walks.

The problem was that they would practically pull my arm off.

So I did what a lot of people do: I went online looking for an answer.

I started experimenting with different training techniques, saw them actually work, and became fascinated by the process.

That curiosity kept growing.

I eventually trained both dogs for off-leash reliability, continued learning with my partially disabled Golden Retriever, and later adopted a high-energy Husky/Boxer mix who had spent about a year struggling to get adopted from a shelter.

He pushed me much deeper into training.

I invested in dog-training education, studied dog behavior and learning theory, took private lessons with an experienced trainer, and spent several months gaining hands-on experience around private lessons and group classes.

Eventually, friends and family started telling me the same thing:

**You should teach people how to do this.**

And that part turned out to be what I enjoyed most.

### I'm here to coach the person holding the leash.

One of the most important things to understand about working with me is that I'm not here to become your dog's trainer forever.

You're their owner.

You're the person who lives with them every day.

So my job is to give you the game plan, explain why we're doing what we're doing, demonstrate it, watch you practice it, and help you improve.

I'm constantly working on making complicated training concepts easier to understand because one of the best things a client could ever tell me is:

**"That finally makes sense."**

I want you to leave this process with skills you can continue using long after our lessons are over.

### And yes, I'm a dog-training nerd.

What started with trying to stop two dogs from dragging me around the neighborhood turned into years of experimenting, studying, training my own dogs, and thinking way too much about why dogs do what they do.

I still enjoy the learning process just as much as I did when I first went down that rabbit hole.

The difference now is that I get to help other owners skip some of the guessing I went through.

# Services Page

## The Calm Companion Program

### Private dog training for a calmer, easier life together.

For families overwhelmed by their dog's chaotic behavior, the **Calm Companion Program** is an eight-session private training program designed to help turn your dog from a daily source of stress into a calmer, more enjoyable part of the family.

Together, we'll build clearer communication, better everyday behavior, and a relationship where you can increasingly **trust your dog to make the right choices.**

Every program is tailored to your dog, your goals, and your real life.

There is no cookie-cutter curriculum.

### The problem we're solving

The Calm Companion Program is designed for owners dealing with everyday behavioral problems such as:

- Pulling on leash
- Chaotic or overly excitable behavior
- Difficulty settling
- Mild-to-moderate dog reactivity
- Poor attention around distractions
- Difficulty communicating clear expectations
- Feeling like you're constantly managing your dog

These issues may not sound extreme on paper.

But when you're dealing with them every day, they add up.

A walk becomes stressful.

Guests become stressful.

Seeing another dog becomes stressful.

Even relaxing at home can become more difficult than it should be.

The program is designed to change those everyday patterns.

### Who it's for

This program is a good fit for the owner who is thinking:

**"I know my dog is capable of better. I just don't know how to get there."**

You don't need previous training experience.

You do need to be willing to participate.

This is hands-on private coaching. I'll teach you what to do, explain why we're doing it, and help you improve—but the progress ultimately comes from the relationship **you** build with your dog.

The program is not intended for severe behavioral cases involving issues such as serious human-directed aggression, dog fighting, or other dangerous behavior.

### What we're really working toward

The emotional goal is **relief**.

I want you to reach the point where you're no longer constantly wondering:

*"What's my dog going to do?"*

Instead, you understand your dog.

You know how to communicate with them.

You know how to handle problems when they appear.

And little by little, you start trusting them to make better decisions.

That confidence changes the way everyday life feels.

### What you'll receive

The Calm Companion Program includes **eight private training sessions**, generally scheduled no more than once per week.

Whenever possible, we train where the behavior actually matters.

That may mean:

- Your home
- Your neighborhood
- Your normal walking route
- A nearby park
- Another location you regularly visit with your dog

If the problem happens on your neighborhood walk, that's where we should work on it.

If the problem happens inside your house, that's where we should work on it.

Training should transfer into your real life—not exist only during a lesson.

You'll also receive:

- Written notes following sessions
- Supporting training videos when useful
- Text and email support for questions between lessons

### What you'll learn

Rather than handing you a checklist of commands, we'll work on the skills your particular dog and household actually need.

That may include things like:

- Clearer communication
- Leash handling
- Engagement and attention
- Calmer walking
- Better choices around distractions
- Appropriate outlets for energy
- Household boundaries and expectations
- Helping your dog settle
- Managing and improving mild-to-moderate reactivity
- Understanding when and how to intervene when your dog makes a poor choice

Most importantly, you'll learn **why** you're doing what you're doing.

The goal is for the training to make sense to you—not for you to blindly follow instructions.

### The outcome

Eight sessions aren't about creating a dog who performs perfectly for a trainer.

They're about giving **you** a framework you can continue using after the program is finished.

The goal is that life starts feeling different:

**Less embarrassment.**  
**Less worrying.**  
**Less managing.**  
**More confidence.**  
**More trust.**  
**More freedom.**

Because when communication becomes clearer, trust has a chance to grow.

**Clarity builds trust. Trust creates freedom.**
